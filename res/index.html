Research Interests

My research focuses on nucleosynthesis and nuclear astrophysics: how chemical elements are produced in stars, stellar explosions, and compact-object environments, and how their abundances reflect the underlying physics.

Solar-system elemental and isotopic abundances provide important benchmarks for tracing the chemical history of the Galaxy. I study the nuclear reactions and decays that shape these abundances, using numerical simulations and uncertainty analyses, often in collaboration with researchers in experimental nuclear physics and observational astrophysics.

Research Topics

Explosive nucleosynthesis in supernovae

Production of iron-group and radioactive nuclides in core-collapse supernovae, and the sensitivity of 56Ni production to helium-burning reactions in pair-instability supernovae. A key question is how nuclear reaction-rate uncertainties propagate into predicted yields.

X-ray bursts and neutron stars

Thermonuclear X-ray bursts and superbursts on accreting neutron stars, including the rp-process, burst ashes, and the effects of neutron-star structure and cooling. This work connects nucleosynthesis calculations with observations from missions such as NinjaSat and MAXI.

Heavy-element nucleosynthesis

The r-, s-, p- and νp-processes in stellar and explosive environments. Topics include magnetorotational supernovae, neutron-star mergers, and the identification of nuclear reactions that most strongly affect the production of heavy elements.

Nuclear fission, reactions, and decays

Nuclear-physics inputs needed for astrophysical simulations, including neutron-rich nuclei, beta decays, and fission. For fission, I have studied post-fission properties using a hybrid approach combining Langevin dynamics with the Hauser–Feshbach statistical model.

Nuclear-physics uncertainties

Monte Carlo variations of reaction rates and other nuclear inputs to quantify abundance uncertainties and identify key reactions. Applications span the s-, p- and νp-processes as well as supernova nucleosynthesis.

Galactic chemical evolution

Connections between nucleosynthetic yields, the enrichment of metal-poor stars, and the chemical evolution of the Galaxy, including the role of neutron-star mergers and magnetorotational supernovae.

Approach and Methods

  • Nuclear reaction-network calculations and stellar/explosion models
  • Monte Carlo sensitivity studies and uncertainty quantification
  • Nuclear fission dynamics and statistical decay calculations
  • Comparison with astronomical observations and experimental nuclear data
